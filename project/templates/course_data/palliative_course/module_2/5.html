Unit Summary

In this unit, you have covered what happens after a person dies and the legal framework surrounding end-of-life care:

  • Death must be formally verified before last offices — the respectful care of a person's body — can take place, taking into account the person's cultural and religious wishes.
  • A number of formal steps follow a death, including documentation, notifying relevant people, and referring the death to the coroner where required.
  • Grief is a natural, individual process, and care workers can support bereaved families through clear, kind communication, practical guidance, and signposting to further bereavement support where needed.
  • The Mental Capacity Act 2005 guides decision-making for people who may lack capacity, and tools such as Advance Decisions and Lasting Power of Attorney allow people to plan ahead.
  • Confidentiality continues to apply after death, and information should only be shared with those who have a legitimate need to know.

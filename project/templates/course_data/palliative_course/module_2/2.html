Verifying and Confirming Death

Death must be formally verified by a suitably qualified professional, such as a nurse or doctor, following local policy. A care worker should never assume a person has died — this must always be confirmed by the appropriate professional and recorded accordingly.

Last Offices

"Last offices" is the term used for the care given to a person's body after death. It is carried out with the same dignity and respect shown throughout the person's life, and typically includes:

  • Washing and dressing the body according to the person's or family's wishes, and any cultural or religious requirements.
  • Removing or securing medical equipment as appropriate.
  • Positioning the body appropriately, and managing any tubes, dressings or personal items sensitively.
  • Preparing the body for transfer, in line with organisational policy.

Wherever possible, family members should be asked whether they wish to be involved in, or present for, this process.

Documentation and Notification

After a death, a number of formal steps must be completed:

  • Recording the time and circumstances of death accurately in the person's records.
  • Notifying the relevant people — the next of kin, GP, and other professionals involved in the person's care.
  • Following organisational procedures for the safe handling of the person's belongings.
  • Arranging for a doctor to complete the Medical Certificate of Cause of Death where required, or referring the death to the coroner if the circumstances require it.

Cultural and Religious Considerations

Practices around death vary widely between cultures and faiths — for example, some traditions require the body to be handled only by people of the same gender, or require burial to take place within a short period of time. Wherever possible, these wishes should be identified and recorded in advance as part of care planning, and always respected after death.

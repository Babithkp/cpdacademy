Mental Capacity and Best Interests

The Mental Capacity Act 2005 sets out how decisions should be made for people who may lack the capacity to make a particular decision for themselves. It is built on key principles:

  • A person must be assumed to have capacity unless it is established otherwise.
  • People must be given all practicable support to make their own decision before it is concluded they cannot.
  • A person is not to be treated as unable to make a decision merely because they make an unwise one.
  • Any decision made on someone's behalf must be in their best interests.
  • Any decision made for someone should be the least restrictive option available.

Advance Decisions and Lasting Power of Attorney

People can plan ahead for a time when they may lose capacity:

  • Advance Decision to Refuse Treatment (ADRT): a legally binding statement, made while a person has capacity, refusing specific treatments in future circumstances — for example, refusing resuscitation.
  • Lasting Power of Attorney (LPA) for Health and Welfare: allows a person to appoint someone they trust to make health and care decisions on their behalf if they later lose capacity.

Both of these must be respected by care staff once they are known to apply.

Death Certification and the Coroner

A doctor who has treated the person during their final illness will usually complete the Medical Certificate of Cause of Death. A death must be referred to the coroner in certain circumstances, such as when the cause of death is unknown, unexpected, or related to an accident or industrial disease. Understanding when a referral is required — and who is responsible for making it — helps ensure the correct process is followed.

Confidentiality and Record Keeping

A person's right to confidentiality does not end when they die. Care workers must continue to handle personal information about the deceased in line with data protection law and organisational policy, sharing details only with those who have a legitimate need to know, such as other professionals involved in the person's care or, where appropriate, the family.

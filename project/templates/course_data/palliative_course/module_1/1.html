Welcome to Palliative Care

Caring for people who are terminally ill or approaching the end of their life is one of the most emotionally demanding areas of health and social care. It calls for trained, compassionate caregivers who can support patients and families with sensitivity, skill, and respect for the person's dignity and wishes.

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this unit, you will be able to:

  • Describe the holistic aspect of caregiving and outline its key elements.
  • Explain what palliative and end-of-life care mean, and compare the two approaches.
  • Identify the various types and stages of illness experienced by terminally ill patients.
  • Discuss the strategies and processes used to ensure meticulous care planning and routine.
  • Explain how to provide appropriate, quality care to patients reaching the end of their lives.

Who This Unit Is For

This unit is relevant to anyone working in a care home, hospice, hospital ward, or home care setting who may be involved in supporting a person nearing the end of their life, as well as those wishing to build their knowledge before moving into this area of work.

Next, you will learn about Understanding Palliative and End-of-Life Care — what each term means, how they differ, and why a holistic approach to care matters.

What Is Palliative Care?

Palliative care is an approach that improves the quality of life of patients and their families facing a life-limiting illness. It focuses on preventing and relieving suffering — physical, emotional, social and spiritual — rather than curing the underlying condition. Palliative care can begin as soon as a serious diagnosis is made and may continue for months or years alongside other treatment.

What Is End-of-Life Care?

End-of-life care is a specific part of palliative care that is provided in the final weeks, days or hours of a person's life, once it is recognised that they are approaching death. It focuses on:

  • Keeping the person as comfortable and pain-free as possible.
  • Supporting their emotional, spiritual and psychological wellbeing.
  • Respecting their wishes about where and how they wish to be cared for.
  • Supporting the family and loved ones through the process.

Comparing the Two

The key difference is timing and scope. Palliative care can be offered at any stage of a serious illness, sometimes for years, and can run alongside curative treatment. End-of-life care is the final phase of palliative care, focused specifically on the period when death is expected in the near future.

The Holistic Approach

Good palliative and end-of-life care is holistic — it looks at the whole person, not just their physical symptoms. This typically covers four connected areas:

  • Physical needs: pain and symptom control, nutrition, hydration, mobility and personal care.
  • Emotional and psychological needs: fear, anxiety, depression, and the process of coming to terms with dying.
  • Social needs: relationships with family and friends, and practical matters such as finances or care of dependents.
  • Spiritual needs: a person's beliefs, values and sense of meaning, which may or may not be religious.

A holistic assessment considers all four areas together, since distress in one area — such as unmanaged pain — often affects the others.

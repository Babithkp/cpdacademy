Livestock Management

Livestock management involves the care, feeding, breeding, and health management of farm animals. This topic covers the key principles for maintaining a productive and healthy herd or flock.

Types of Livestock

Common livestock on farms include:

  • Cattle: Raised for beef, dairy, or both. Proper herd management includes nutrition planning, breeding programmes, and veterinary care.
  • Sheep: Kept for wool, meat, and milk. Flock management requires attention to grazing land quality and parasite control.
  • Poultry: Chickens, ducks, and turkeys raised for eggs and meat. Housing, ventilation, and biosecurity are critical factors.
  • Pigs: Raised primarily for meat. Key considerations include feed efficiency, housing, and waste management.

Animal Nutrition

Proper nutrition is essential for animal health and productivity. Livestock diets must include:

  • Energy: Provided by grains and forages, energy fuels daily activities and growth.
  • Protein: Essential for muscle growth and milk production, sourced from legumes and oil seed meals.
  • Vitamins and Minerals: Supplements may be needed to prevent deficiencies, particularly in housed livestock.
  • Water: Clean, fresh water must always be available. Requirements vary by species and stage of production.

Animal Health and Welfare

Maintaining animal health requires a proactive approach:

  • Vaccination Programmes: Protecting livestock from common diseases through regular vaccination schedules.
  • Parasite Control: Implementing strategic worming programmes based on faecal egg counts.
  • Housing Standards: Providing adequate space, ventilation, and bedding to promote welfare.
  • Record Keeping: Documenting health events, treatments, and breeding records for each animal.

Breeding and Genetics

Modern breeding programmes use both natural mating and artificial insemination (AI) to improve desirable traits such as growth rate, milk yield, disease resistance, and feed efficiency. Genetic testing can help farmers make informed breeding decisions.

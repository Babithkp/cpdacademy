Adverse Weather and Poor Road Conditions

The UK's variable weather means that drivers must be prepared for a wide range of conditions. This topic explains how to adapt your driving to stay safe in rain, fog, ice, snow, and other challenging situations.

Driving in Rain

  • Reduce Speed: Wet roads significantly increase stopping distances. Allow at least double the normal following distance.
  • Use Dipped Headlights: Improve your visibility and make it easier for other drivers to see you.
  • Beware of Aquaplaning: If you feel the steering become light, ease off the accelerator and avoid sudden braking or steering.
  • Watch for Floods: Never attempt to drive through deep water. If you must drive through a shallow flood, use first gear and keep the revs high.

Driving in Fog

  • Use Fog Lights: Switch on front and rear fog lights when visibility drops below 100 metres. Remember to switch them off when visibility improves.
  • Reduce Speed Significantly: You should be able to stop well within the distance you can see clearly.
  • Use the Left Kerb: As a guide to your road position, rather than the centre line which could draw you towards oncoming traffic.
  • Listen for Traffic: Open your window at junctions and crossroads to listen for approaching vehicles.

Driving in Ice and Snow

  • Gentle Inputs: Accelerate, brake, and steer gently. Sudden movements can cause loss of traction.
  • Higher Gears: Pull away in second gear to reduce wheel spin.
  • Stopping Distances: On ice, stopping distances can be ten times greater than on dry roads.
  • Black Ice: Watch for shiny, smooth-looking road surfaces, especially on bridges, overpasses, and shaded areas.

Driving in Strong Winds

  • Give extra space to high-sided vehicles, motorcyclists, and cyclists who may be blown off course.
  • Be cautious when passing gaps in hedges, buildings, or bridges where crosswinds can catch you unexpectedly.

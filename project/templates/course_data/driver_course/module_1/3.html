Pre-Journey Safety Checks

Before setting off on any journey, a responsible driver should carry out pre-journey checks to ensure the vehicle is safe and roadworthy. This topic covers the essential inspections you should perform.

The POWER Check

A simple way to remember your pre-journey checks is the acronym POWER:

  • P — Petrol (Fuel): Ensure you have enough fuel for your journey. Running out of fuel on a motorway or busy road is dangerous and an offence on some roads.
  • O — Oil: Check the engine oil level using the dipstick. Low oil can cause engine damage and breakdowns.
  • W — Water: Check the coolant level and windscreen washer fluid. Overheating can cause serious engine damage.
  • E — Electrics: Test all lights (headlights, brake lights, indicators, hazard lights), horn, and windscreen wipers.
  • R — Rubber: Inspect tyres for correct pressure, adequate tread depth (minimum 1.6mm across the central three-quarters), and any visible damage. Also check windscreen wiper blades.

Additional Checks

  • Mirrors: Ensure all mirrors are clean and properly adjusted for optimum visibility.
  • Seatbelts: Confirm all seatbelts are functioning correctly. All passengers must wear seatbelts by law.
  • Windscreen: Check for cracks or chips that may impair visibility. Damage larger than 40mm in the driver's field of vision is an MOT failure.
  • Load Security: If carrying luggage or equipment, ensure it is properly secured and does not obstruct visibility or movement.

UK-Specific Driving Preparations

When driving in the UK, be prepared for:

  • Driving on the left-hand side of the road.
  • Navigating roundabouts — give way to traffic from the right.
  • Urban driving with narrow streets, parked cars, and heavy pedestrian traffic.
  • Motorway driving — check lane discipline, speed limits, and hard shoulder rules.

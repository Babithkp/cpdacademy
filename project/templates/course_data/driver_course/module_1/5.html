Mental and Physical Well-being for Drivers

Your mental and physical state have a direct impact on your ability to drive safely. This topic explores the factors that affect driver fitness and what you can do to ensure you are always in the best condition to drive.

Driver Fatigue

Fatigue is a major cause of road accidents. Key facts and tips:

  • Warning Signs: Heavy eyelids, frequent yawning, difficulty concentrating, drifting across lanes, and forgetting the last few miles driven.
  • Prevention: Get a good night's sleep before a long journey. Take a 15-minute break every two hours. Avoid driving between midnight and 6am when your body clock naturally dips.
  • Emergency Measures: If you feel drowsy, stop at a safe place, drink two cups of coffee or a caffeinated drink, then take a 15-20 minute nap to allow the caffeine to take effect.

Alcohol and Drugs

  • UK Legal Limits: The drink-drive limit in England, Wales, and Northern Ireland is 80mg of alcohol per 100ml of blood. In Scotland, it is 50mg. However, the safest option is not to drink at all before driving.
  • Effects: Alcohol slows reaction times, impairs judgement, reduces coordination, and creates a false sense of confidence.
  • Medication: Some prescription and over-the-counter medicines can cause drowsiness or impair driving ability. Always check the label and consult your doctor or pharmacist.
  • Illegal Drugs: It is illegal to drive under the influence of illegal drugs. The law specifies limits for a number of controlled substances.

Eyesight Requirements

By law, you must be able to read a number plate from 20 metres (approximately 5 car lengths) away. If you need glasses or contact lenses to meet this standard, you must always wear them while driving.

Accident Preparedness

Every vehicle should carry essential items in case of an accident or breakdown:

  • A warning triangle
  • A high-visibility vest
  • A first aid kit
  • A fully charged mobile phone
  • A torch with spare batteries
  • A fire extinguisher (recommended)

If involved in an accident, you must stop, switch off the engine, turn on hazard lights, and call the emergency services if anyone is injured. Exchange details with other parties involved.

Road Signals and Markings

Understanding road signals and markings is essential for safe driving and efficient traffic flow. This topic covers the main types of road signs used in the UK and what they mean for drivers.

Types of Road Signs

Road signs in the UK are divided into three main categories:

  • Warning Signs: Triangular signs with a red border that alert drivers to potential hazards ahead, such as sharp bends, crossroads, or roadworks.
  • Regulatory Signs: Circular signs that give orders and must be obeyed. Red circles indicate prohibitions (e.g., no entry, speed limits). Blue circles give positive instructions (e.g., minimum speed, direction).
  • Information Signs: Rectangular signs that provide helpful information about routes, distances, and facilities. Green backgrounds indicate primary routes; blue backgrounds indicate motorways; white backgrounds indicate local routes.

Road Markings

Road markings provide important guidance to drivers:

  • White Dashed Lines: Separate lanes of traffic travelling in the same direction. Longer dashes indicate a hazard ahead.
  • White Solid Lines: Indicate that crossing or straddling is not permitted, often used on dangerous bends or near junctions.
  • Yellow Lines: Single yellow lines mean no waiting during restricted hours. Double yellow lines mean no waiting at any time.
  • Zigzag Lines: Found near pedestrian crossings and indicate no parking or overtaking.
  • Hatched Markings: Diagonal lines within a boundary to separate traffic flows or protect turning vehicles.

Traffic Lights

The standard traffic light sequence in the UK is:

  • Red: Stop and wait behind the stop line.
  • Red and Amber: Get ready to go but do not move yet.
  • Green: Go if the way is clear.
  • Amber: Stop unless you have already crossed the stop line or are so close that stopping might cause an accident.
